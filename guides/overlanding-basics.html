OVERLANDING 101

Overlanding Basics

Start with reliability and self-sufficiency, not accessories. Your first setup should help you travel, sleep, eat and deal with common problems without turning the vehicle into an overloaded gear catalog.

Vehicle first

Make sure maintenance, tires, fluids and the spare are sorted before buying expedition gear. Know your vehicle's payload and avoid loading equipment simply because it looks useful.

Navigation & communication

Have an offline navigation option and tell someone your route. Remote travel can mean losing cellular service, so choose communication equipment appropriate to how far from help you plan to travel.

Recovery & repair

Carry equipment you know how to use for the terrain you actually drive. A tire-pressure gauge, tire repair capability, inflator, basic tools and appropriate recovery equipment can be more valuable than cosmetic modifications.

Water, food & shelter

Carry adequate water with a reserve, simple meals, weather-appropriate shelter and a sleep system. Keep the frequently used gear accessible rather than buried beneath the entire loadout.

Build in stages

Take short trips with a simple setup. After every trip, note what you used, what annoyed you and what you never touched. Let actual trips determine the next upgrade.

Trailnest rule: capability before cosmetics.
