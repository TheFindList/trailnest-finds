CAMP COOKING

A Camp Kitchen That Actually Works

The best camp kitchen is organized enough that making coffee or dinner does not require unloading the entire vehicle.

The core cooking bin

Keep a stove and fuel, lighter or fire starter, pot or pan, cooking utensil, sharp knife, cutting board, eating utensils, plates or bowls and mugs together. One grab-and-go kitchen bin makes packing much easier.

Water & cleanup

Bring a refillable water container, wash bin, sponge or scrubber, towels and trash bags. Follow local rules for wastewater and food scraps rather than dumping them around camp.

Food storage

Use a cooler for food that needs refrigeration and sealed containers for dry food. Wildlife rules vary by destination; use required lockers or animal-resistant storage and never leave food unattended where prohibited.

Make meals easier

Prep ingredients at home, choose meals that share ingredients and keep the first night's dinner simple. A few reliable meals beat hauling an oversized kitchen you barely use.

Campfire backup

Do not make the entire meal plan depend on a fire. Restrictions can change. A camp stove gives you a cooking option when fires are prohibited; always check current fire rules for the destination.

Trailnest rule: organize around the meal, not around owning more cookware.
